# What makes a story worth a post

This file decides which stories get shortlisted and how they are scored. It
does not decide how posts are written. That is style_guide.md.

---

## 1. What these posts are for

In priority order:

**Credibility that warms cold outbound.** The primary job. A prospect who gets
a cold email checks the profile before replying. A feed of specific, sourced,
useful posts is what converts "who is this" into "worth a call". This means
posts must survive being read by a skeptical senior person who has never heard
of the author.

**Inbound leads from buyers.** Secondary but real. A post lands with someone
already mid-decision and they reach out.

**Partnerships and channel.** GSIs, consultancies and platform partners
evaluating who can deliver for them. They read the same delivery and governance
stories buyers do, so this rarely pulls in a different direction.

Not a goal: recruiting. Do not score stories up because they would appeal to
engineers looking for work.

The consequence for scoring: a story is worth a post if it would make a
skeptical senior buyer think "this person understands my problem". Not if it
would get engagement.

---

## 2. Who is reading

**Primary: senior buyers at upper mid-market companies, roughly 500 to 5,000
staff, in the US and UK.**

That size is the sharpest thing in this document, so calibrate against it.
These companies do not have fifty-person AI teams or a signed Accenture
contract. They have a handful of good engineers, a long backlog, and a board
asking about AI. Their problems are capacity, sequencing, build versus buy, and
what breaks when a lean team ships something into a regulated process.

Score down stories that only make sense at Fortune 100 scale: multi-thousand
person transformation programs, nine-figure platform commitments, internal
research labs. Interesting, but not the reader's world.

Titles that matter: CIO, CTO, Chief Data Officer, VP Engineering, Head of AI,
Head of Data, Head of Digital, COO.

At capital, wealth and asset management firms, "CIO" means Chief Investment
Officer, not Chief Information Officer. Not a buyer.

**Secondary: GSIs and consultancies** evaluating delivery partners, and **other
founders and agency peers**. Write for the primary reader. The secondary
audiences read the same material, and if a post is sharp for a CIO it is
usually interesting to them too. Never write a post aimed at peers.

**Sectors:** healthcare, insurance, banking and lending, legal, logistics,
property, media. Document-heavy and regulated.

---

## 3. Verticals do not drive scoring

Deliberate, and worth understanding before scoring anything.

A post that opens "insurers are facing" is invisible to the banking and
healthcare half of the audience. The pattern that works is a horizontal
argument on vertical evidence: the story comes from one industry, the lesson
applies to all of them, and the closing question names sectors so buyers
recognize themselves.

Do not score a story up because it came from insurance or down because it came
from developer tooling. Score it on whether it supports an argument a
regulated-industry buyer would recognize as true. Feed selection already
handles vertical coverage.

---

## 4. The job of a post

Translate news into what it means for buyers.

The reader saw the headline and has not worked out the implication for their own
roadmap. The post does that work. It is not commentary, not analysis for its own
sake, not an explainer.

Test: after reading, could the reader do something differently in a decision
they are already facing? If not, the story is not worth a post.

Be contrarian only when the evidence is hard. A well-supported disagreement with
the consensus is the strongest post available. A contrarian position taken for
its own sake is worse than a useful obvious one, because the primary job is
credibility with skeptical strangers and a stretched take costs exactly that.
When in doubt, be right and useful rather than provocative.

---

## 5. Freshness

**The hook must be news from the last 72 hours.** Not negotiable. Older
material may be used as supporting context inside the post, but the thing the
post is reacting to must be current.

A story published four days ago is not a hook, however good it is. Someone
posting about last week's news reads as slow, which is the opposite of the
credibility this is meant to build.

Reports, surveys and analysis pieces count as news if published in the window.

---

## 6. Where the story comes from

Prefer stories about US and UK organizations, since that is where the readers
are and a domestic example lands harder.

Stories from elsewhere are fine when the lesson travels. A payments framework
built in Asia by Visa and Mastercard is relevant to a UK bank. A regulatory
development specific to one non-target market usually is not.

Score down when the reader would have to do translation work to see why it
matters to them.

---

## 7. Story types, in priority order

Score toward the top of the range for categories nearer the top.

**1. How companies are actually buying and building.**
The strongest category. Real decisions by real organizations that reveal what
the market has concluded. A company buying a capability instead of building it.
A consultancy staffing engineers against a platform. An enterprise rolling
something back. A buyer switching approach or vendor. These are signals a
reader uses as evidence about their own choices, and most people posting will
only repeat the headline number.

**2. Governance, security and compliance.**
What regulated buyers lose sleep over. Agent permissions, egress and
credentials, evaluation and audit trails, model and vendor risk, identity and
access for agents, incoming rules with operational consequences. Score high
when there is a concrete incident or a named framework. Score low when it is
abstract policy commentary.

**3. Production failures and postmortems.**
Someone shipped it, it broke, they wrote it up honestly. Usually contested,
which means there is an argument to make. Score high when the organization is
one the reader respects, because "even they could not do it" carries the point.

**4. Technical architecture and how it works.**
Lowest priority. Score high only when the technical detail changes a buying
decision, not when it is interesting to engineers.

---

## 8. What makes a story arguable

The best stories have a gap between the obvious take and the true one. Ask:

- What will most people posting about this say today?
- Is there a more useful reading the obvious take misses?
- Can this author make that reading without leaving the delivery lane in
  section 10?

If the answer to the second question is no, the story is reportable but not
postable. Cap it at 5. A post that repeats the consensus is not worth the
reader's attention or the author's name on it.

---

## 9. Never post about these

Hard exclusions. Score 1 and do not shortlist, regardless of how good the
story is.

- **Layoffs and AI replacing jobs.** No upside. Every take has been made, and
  the ones that land are either callous or naive.
- **Politics and regulatory fights.** Regulation that changes what a buyer must
  build is in scope under section 7.2. The political argument about it is not.
- **Competitors and peer agencies.** Never comment on another services firm,
  favorably or otherwise. Peers are an audience, not a subject.

AI safety, lab drama and researcher departures are not banned but are almost
never postable, because they do not touch a buying decision. Treat as a 3 or 4
unless the story has a concrete operational consequence for enterprises.

---

## 10. What the author can credibly claim

Use this when a post touches on what the team does. Anything outside this list
must not be claimed. The style guide governs how rarely to mention it at all.

**Latent IT.** AI-native product engineering, senior only, founding team
involved in every engagement. Technopark, Thiruvananthapuram. Engagement
models: fixed-scope MVP, dedicated AI pod, project or retainer.

**Shipped in production by members of the founding team:**

- Document AI and intelligent document processing
- RAG and knowledge assistants
- Computer vision
- MLOps, evaluation and monitoring

Also in the lane: AIOps, IoT, data engineering, intelligent process automation.

**Credibility anchor.** Harish co-founded and scaled Perfomatix, acquired by
VRIZE in 2023. Work delivered before Latent IT existed is attributed as
"delivered by members of our founding team", never to VRIZE or Latent IT.

Never invent a client name, an engagement, a metric or an outcome. If a post
needs a specific delivery example that is not in the research or in this
section, write the post without it.

---

## 11. Score low

- Model releases, benchmarks, parameter counts, context window claims
- Funding rounds where the number is the only story. A raise becomes
  interesting when what the company *did* with it reveals something
- Chip, GPU and datacenter capacity news
- Consumer AI, AI art, image and video generation
- Developer tooling with no enterprise buying implication
- Macro takes on AI and GDP, or the bubble question
- Anything requiring speculation outside the delivery lane in section 10
- Vendor content marketing dressed as research
- Stories where a thousand people will post the same take today
- Anything on the recently offered list
- Anything that only makes sense at Fortune 100 scale

---

## 12. Sourcing

- Reject anything with no identifiable primary source
- Reject anything sourced only to vendor marketing
- Prefer company newsrooms, regulator publications, research firm reports and
  established trade press over aggregators and SEO content
- Name the source of every statistic in key_facts, and flag it when the
  publisher has a commercial interest in the number
- Where a claim is reported rather than confirmed, say so in key_facts so the
  hedge survives into the draft

---

## 13. Scoring calibration

Use the whole range. Honest scoring is what makes the numbers useful.

- **9** A story where a genuinely better argument exists that almost nobody
  else will make, with hard sourcing, landing in category 1 or 2.
- **8** Strong story, clear argument, good sources. Worth posting today.
- **7** Good story, the argument is solid but not surprising.
- **6** Reportable. A competent post is possible but it will not stand out.
- **5** Weak. Either the argument is thin or the sourcing is soft.
- **4 or below** Should not be posted.

If everything is scoring 8 or 9, the scoring is broken. Most days produce one
or two stories above 7.

**On a thin day, still return the best available even if nothing clears 6.**
The author would rather see a 5 and decide for himself than receive nothing.
Score it honestly at 5, explain in score_reasoning why it is weak, and let him
judge. Never inflate a score to make a thin day look better. The scores are only
useful if they are true.

## 14. India audience supplement

This brief serves an India-based professional tech audience. Apply these
adjustments on top of the base scoring rules above.

**Score up:**
- India startup funding, product launches, and IPOs when there is an
  argument beyond the number. A Series B is not a story; what the product
  does and who the buyer is may be.
- Government tech policy with operational consequence: DPDP Act compliance,
  Digital India initiatives, PLI scheme outcomes, ONDC adoption.
- India-first product launches from global companies (Apple India pricing,
  Google Pay India features, LinkedIn India workforce data).
- Indian IT services firms (TCS, Infosys, Wipro, HCL) deploying or buying
  AI at scale, especially where execution detail is available.
- Mobile-first and UPI-adjacent tech, since India's digital infrastructure
  is the context for most enterprise decisions here.

**Score down:**
- Stories relevant only to US/UK regulatory context with no India angle.
- Stories about layoffs at Indian IT companies: the take has been made
  a thousand times and it damages credibility with the audience.
- Consumer gadget launches without a professional or enterprise angle.

**Geography note:**
Prefer stories where the primary actor or primary impact is in India or
explicitly relevant to the Indian tech decision-maker. A global AI story
with a clear "what this means for Indian enterprises" angle scores as well
as a domestic story. A global story with no India angle scores normally
under the base rules.
